# Day 3 lab: make a response card

[中文](README.zh.md) · [English starter](starter/response-plan.md) · [English example solution](solution/response-plan.md)

This is a paper exercise. The three events are **separate fictional hypothetical branches** based on Grace's cases; they do not change the outcomes of C1–C4 on Day 1. The exercise does not describe a real incident, a real payment, or completed real-world action. Do not actually contact an institution, call, pay, access an account, install or download software, click a suspicious link, or upload identity information, codes, or real materials. AI is not needed. If a real incident occurs, seek help without completing the exercise first.

## Three hypotheses

- **A: a code has been disclosed.** In an alternative fictional version of C3, Grace told an unverified “support agent” a one-time code and then became suspicious. She has not seen a transfer, but does not know whether the code was used. No real code is provided.
- **B: a payment has been completed.** In a separate fictional version, Grace suspects a scam after transferring US$12,000 to a “safe account.” This is an imagined exercise only: no real transfer or outcome occurred, and it does not imply A or C happened.
- **C: unfamiliar remote-access software was installed.** In a third independent hypothesis, Grace followed the caller's instruction to install unfamiliar remote-access software and enabled screen sharing. She does not know what the other party saw. No software was actually installed and this does not imply A or B happened.

## Steps

1. Open [starter/response-plan.md](starter/response-plan.md). For **each** of A, B, and C, write the known and unknown facts, what to stop, how to contact the bank or payment provider promptly through a known or independently verified official channel, how to contact local police, and how a trusted person can help.
2. For A, explain why a disclosed code still needs prompt reporting even without a visible transfer and must not be read out again. For B, identify the payment method, ask about stopping, recalling, or other options, and say that results are not guaranteed. For C, stop screen sharing and the other party's connection, use another trusted device first to contact institutions and police, and follow institutional or trusted professional recovery guidance; do not wait for device repair before contacting the financial institution.
3. List records that can be safely kept, help that evidence collection must not delay, and a response to an advance-fee recovery promise. Then write and rehearse a short response card. Working alone is fine.
4. Check the list below, then compare the [example solution](solution/response-plan.md). Procedures vary by location, institution, and case; other safe, reasonable answers are valid.

## Acceptance checks

- [ ] Treat the three scenarios as independent fiction. If someone may face immediate physical danger, contact local emergency services immediately via a trusted route; do not wait for verification, screenshots, or a family reply.
- [ ] Stop further disclosure or payment. Contact the bank or payment provider and local police promptly through known or independently verified official routes; act in a locally appropriate order or in parallel without waiting for one institution before contacting the other.
- [ ] A does not assume safety from the absence of a visible transfer. B asks about stopping, recalling, or other options without a guarantee. C stops sharing and the connection, uses another trusted device first for help, and follows institutional or trusted professional recovery guidance.
- [ ] Keep appropriate records when safe and without delaying help; name how a trusted person can assist; refuse an advance-fee “recovery” promise and do not disclose another code or grant remote access.
- [ ] No real contacts, calls, transfers, software installation or downloads, suspicious-link clicks, or uploads of identity information, codes, or real materials occurred.

## Official references and region

- Mainland China: [Government of China: emergency numbers 110, 119, and 120](https://www.gov.cn/ztzl/yjzn/content_562406.htm); [Beijing Communications Administration: 110 for police and 120 for emergency medical assistance](https://bjca.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/tzgg/art/2022/art_8d4eb93ee3424f30826c97ee400e8937.html); [Shanghai police on stopping transfers, reporting, contacting the bank, and preserving records](https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/nw31406/20250527/ab65c2b8613447a9bcd8535363369efd.html); [Nanjing police on 110 reporting and the role of 96110](https://gaj.nanjing.gov.cn/njsgaj/202103/t20210331_2865220.html); [Datong police: 96110 is not a police emergency reporting number](https://www.dt.gov.cn/dtszf/gafwzsks/202609/d79aab3841ca4438963358198a89eed6.shtml); [Nantong police on advance-fee recovery scams](https://gaj.nantong.gov.cn/ntsgaj/jfts/content/6508399c-4be5-4fee-92b4-e47072f42555.html). In mainland China, use 110 to report to police and 120 for emergency medical assistance; 96110 provides anti-fraud warning, consultation, and tip reporting, and does not replace 110.
- United States: [911.gov: emergency police, fire, and medical help](https://www.911.gov/calling-911/); [FBI IC3 FAQ: call 911 or local police for immediate danger and contact local law enforcement directly for time-sensitive matters](https://www.ic3.gov/Home/FAQ); [FTC advice after a scam involving payment, information, or device access](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed). Procedures differ by payment method; US contacts and numbers in that source are not mainland China hotlines.

Elsewhere, use independently verified police, emergency-service, and financial-institution channels appropriate to the location. No institution can guarantee that a payment will be stopped or money recovered.
