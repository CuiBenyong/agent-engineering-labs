# Day 3 example solution: three hypotheses and a response card

Everything below is a **fictional paper simulation**: no real code was disclosed, payment made, software installed, institution contacted, or response carried out. A, B, and C are independent and do not change the Day 1 outcomes. Other reasonable answers that seek prompt help and verify independently are welcome.

**Shared priority:** If anyone may be in immediate physical danger, contact local emergency services immediately through a trusted route. Do not wait for screenshots, a bank response, a family reply, or the worksheet. The examples below assume no immediate physical danger is currently known; seek emergency help immediately if that changes.

## A: hypothetical disclosure of a code

- **Known / unknown:** In this hypothesis Grace disclosed a one-time code. She sees no transfer, but does not know whether the code was used or the account affected. No visible transfer does not mean safety.
- **Stop and contact:** End the conversation with unverified support; stop further payments, disclosure, or approvals and do not repeat the code. In the simulation she promptly contacts the bank using the card-back number or an independently verified official channel; contact any involved payment provider too. She reports, “I told a one-time code to an unverified person; I have not confirmed a transaction,” and asks it to check and advise on protection without reading out the code or a password.
- **Police and support:** Contact local police promptly as appropriate without waiting for the bank's final response. Ask Mei to accompany her or write down institutional guidance through a trusted route. If immediate physical danger emerges, contact local emergency services immediately.
- **Records / follow-up:** Without delaying contact, note the approximate time, that an unverified agent was involved, and the guidance received. Do not publish real records or upload them to AI. Follow the institution's account and authorisation checks and protective instructions; whether further loss can be prevented is unknown.

## B: hypothetical completed payment

- **Known / unknown:** In this separate hypothesis, Grace sent a fictional US$12,000 bank transfer to the requested “safe account.” The recipient's real identity and whether the payment can be stopped or recovered remain unknown.
- **Stop and contact:** Make no further payment or disclosure. Promptly contact the relevant bank and payment provider via the card-back number or independently verified official channels. Report the suspected scam, payment method, fictional amount, and approximate time; ask about a stop, recall, or other protective options. **Asking does not guarantee a stop or recovery.**
- **Police, records, and support:** Do not wait for the financial institution's outcome before contacting police; act promptly as locally appropriate, in parallel if possible. In mainland China, promptly call **110** to report; **96110** offers consultation or tip reporting but does not replace police reporting. Where safe and without delaying help, keep available transfer receipts and conversation records; ask Mei to note the institution's instructions. If a detail is missing, report what is known now and supply further information via the official route later.
- **Recovery scam:** If an unknown person demands an advance “unlocking fee” to recover funds, refuse payment and give no code or remote access. Verify the claim through the bank or police using an independently confirmed channel. Recovery is not guaranteed.

## C: hypothetical unfamiliar remote-access software

- **Known / unknown:** In this separate hypothesis Grace installed unfamiliar remote-access software and enabled screen sharing. She does not know which information the other party saw or whether accounts were affected.
- **Stop and contact:** Stop screen sharing and end the other party's connection. If control may persist, disconnect that device from the network when doing so does not interfere with personal safety or emergency contact. Stop further payment, disclosure, and following the other party's instructions.
- **Another device and recovery:** Use another trusted device first to contact the bank or payment provider and police promptly via known or independently verified official routes. Explain the installation, sharing, and unknown exposure. Ask Mei to help find a trusted device and record guidance; do not wait for full device repair before contacting the financial institution. Do not type a new password on a possibly controlled device. Follow the institution's or trusted professional's device and account recovery guidance; ask how to retain records before cleanup if needed.
- **Records / outcome:** If safe and without delaying help, record the existing software name, approximate time, or conversation record. Do not reconnect to the other party or open a suspicious link to collect evidence. Any loss and possible recovery remain unknown.

## Example response card

> If anyone may be in immediate physical danger, contact local emergency services immediately. Stop further disclosure and payment and end the interaction controlled by the other party. Promptly contact the bank or payment provider and police using known or independently verified official channels; act in parallel where appropriate. For remote access, stop sharing and the connection, use another trusted device first, and follow recovery guidance. Keep available records where safe without delaying help and ask a trusted person to assist. Refuse advance-fee recovery promises, repeat code requests, and renewed remote access. Outcomes are not guaranteed.

In mainland China use **110** for police reporting and **120** for emergency medical assistance; **96110** provides anti-fraud warnings, consultation, and tip reporting and does not replace 110. Elsewhere use independently confirmed local police and emergency services. These are plans only; no real call was made.
