# Day 1 lab: verify before paying

Chinese instructions: [README.zh.md](README.zh.md). Use the `.zh.md` starter and solution if you prefer Chinese. The cases and acceptance checks match across languages.

All people, numbers, and messages are fictional. If you use an AI assistant, paste only the fictional text below. Do not click real links or call real numbers as part of this exercise.

## The cases (Tuesday 20 October 2026)

Grace is 68 and retired. Her daughter is Mei; Mei's son, Grace's grandson, is Theo. Theo's and Mei's numbers are saved in Grace's phone.

- **C1, a voice call, 21:40.** An unknown number calls. The voice sounds exactly like Theo: “Grandma, I had a car accident and I'm at the police station. Please don't tell Mom. A lawyer will call you.” Minutes later, a “lawyer” calls from another unknown number and asks Grace to buy $3,000 in gift cards tonight and read him the card numbers to cover bail.
- **C2, a text message.** “Your parcel could not be delivered. Pay a $1.99 redelivery fee within 12 hours,” followed by a link to a site that imitates a delivery company. Grace is expecting no parcel she knows of.
- **C3, a fake support line.** Worried about a charge she does not recognise, Grace searches online for her bank's phone number and calls a number shown in a search advert. The “agent” says her account is compromised, tells her to move her $12,000 of savings to a “safe account,” and asks her to read out the one-time code just sent to her phone.
- **C4, a contrast case.** Theo messages from the number already saved in Grace's contacts: “Can you send me a photo of that recipe?”

## Tasks

1. In `starter/verify.md`, mark the five red flags for each case: urgency, secrecy, hard-to-reverse payment, a request for codes or card details, and a channel Grace cannot verify.
2. Rate each case high or low risk, and write the stop, check, and tell steps. Every check must use a channel Grace already has or finds officially.
3. Review the AI assessment below. For each claim, name the problem and write a corrected statement.
4. Write a one-page family verification plan: code word rules, whom to call, a never-do list, and what to do if money or details have already gone.

> AI assessment to review: “C1 sounds like it really is Theo, and the lawyer gave a case number, so it is probably genuine — gift cards are a quick way to pay bail. C2 is only $1.99, so paying is low risk. C3 is safe because Grace found the number herself. C4 is suspicious because it arrived as a text message, so Grace should ignore it.”

## Acceptance checks

- C1, C2, and C3 are high risk, each with at least three red flags named; C4 is low risk.
- Every verification step uses a saved number, a family code word, the number on Grace's bank card, the official app, or an address typed by Grace — never a number or link from the caller, the message, or a search advert.
- No step involves paying, reading out a code, clicking the link, or moving money to “protect” it.
- The family plan covers the code word, whom to call, a never-do list, and the steps after money or details have gone, including calling the bank on its official number and reporting.

Compare your work with `solution/verify.md` after completing the review. Alternative wording is fine when every check stays independent of the scammer's channel.

C4 is lower risk in this exercise, not proof of identity or safety; a saved number can be spoofed, so verify independently if in doubt. If someone may be in immediate physical danger, contact local emergency services immediately through a trusted route; do not wait to finish an exercise.
