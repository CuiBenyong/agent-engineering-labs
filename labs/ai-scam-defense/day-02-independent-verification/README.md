# Day 2 lab: independent verification practice

[中文](README.zh.md) · [English starter](starter/independent-verification.md) · [English example solution](solution/independent-verification.md)

This paper exercise returns to the fictional Grace, her daughter Mei, and her grandson Theo from Day 1. Grace lives in the United States. C1–C4 do not change their original outcomes; the mainland China family scenario below is a separate fictional hypothesis. **Do not actually contact an institution, make a call or payment, download software, click or copy suspicious links, or upload identity information, codes, or real materials.** AI is optional; if used, supply only fictional details. If a real incident is underway, seek help without waiting to finish the exercise.

If someone may be in immediate physical danger, contact local emergency services immediately through a trusted route; do not wait for a code word, a family reply, or a completed worksheet. This exercise makes no actual calls.

## Four cases

- **C1, a family emergency:** At 21:40 an unknown number calls. The voice sounds like Theo. The caller claims there was a car accident and that he is at a police station, and says not to tell his mother. Later, someone claiming to be a lawyer asks Grace to buy **US$3,000 in gift cards** that night and read out the card numbers.
- **C2, a parcel message:** A text claims a parcel could not be delivered and requests **US$1.99** for redelivery within 12 hours. It contains a link imitating a delivery company; Grace does not remember expecting a parcel. No link is provided in this exercise.
- **C3, fake support:** Grace notices an unfamiliar charge and calls a “bank number” from a search advert. The person claims her account has been compromised, asks her to move **US$12,000** to a “safe account,” and requests a one-time code.
- **C4, lower-risk contrast:** A message displayed as coming from Theo's saved number asks for a recipe photo. No payment, code, secrecy, or deadline was observed. A saved number can be spoofed, and a photo can contain private information.

## Steps

1. Open [starter/independent-verification.md](starter/independent-verification.md). For each of C1–C4, record a **direct observation**, an **unverified claim or unknown**, and a **next action**. For example, hearing someone claim an accident is an observation; whether the accident happened is still unknown. A fact confirmed through an independent channel does not authenticate the whole conversation.
2. For C1, C2, and C3, write an interrupting action and an independent verification route: a previously saved family contact and a backup, the delivery company's established official app or an independently verified channel, and the card-back number or independently verified bank channel. Do not use the other party's contact details, the message link, or the search-advert number. For C4, explain its relatively lower risk, check the photo's privacy, and say how to verify if unsure.
3. In the starter, handle a **separate mainland China hypothesis**: a mother receives a call with a voice like her daughter's; the caller claims an accident and demands secrecy and an immediate transfer. Explain what happens if there may be immediate physical danger, how to stop payment and contact family, and the different roles of 110, 120, and 96110. Do not write any private phone number.
4. Practise “stop, check, tell” aloud, alone or with a partner. Check the list before reading the example solution. Different reasonable answers are welcome.

## Acceptance checks

- [ ] All four rows distinguish a specific observation, an unverified claim or unknown, and a next action; a voice, saved number, code word, or lower risk alone does not prove identity or safety.
- [ ] C1–C3 each interrupt the risky action and use an independent route; do not click the text link, give the fake support agent a code, or transfer to a “safe account.” Still check the original bank charge independently.
- [ ] C4 explains why lower risk is not proof of safety, considers photo privacy, and verifies independently if uncertain.
- [ ] For possible immediate physical danger, contact local emergency services immediately without mechanically waiting. In mainland China, **110** is used for police reporting, **120** for emergency medical assistance, and **96110** supports anti-fraud warning and consultation or fraud-tip reporting and does not replace emergency police reporting.
- [ ] No real calls, contact, transfers, downloads, suspicious-link clicks, or uploads of real identity information or one-time codes occurred.

## Official references and region

- Mainland China: [Government of China: emergency numbers 110, 119, and 120](https://www.gov.cn/ztzl/yjzn/content_562406.htm); [Beijing Communications Administration: 110 for police and 120 for emergency medical assistance](https://bjca.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/tzgg/art/2022/art_8d4eb93ee3424f30826c97ee400e8937.html); [Nanjing police on 96110 warning, consultation, and tips](https://gaj.nanjing.gov.cn/njsgaj/202103/t20210331_2865220.html); [Datong police explain that 96110 is not the police emergency reporting number](https://www.dt.gov.cn/dtszf/gafwzsks/202609/d79aab3841ca4438963358198a89eed6.shtml).
- United States: [911.gov: emergency police, fire, and medical help](https://www.911.gov/calling-911/); [FTC guidance on verifying a supposed family emergency](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/scammers-use-ai-enhance-their-family-emergency-schemes). Its independent-callback advice is useful elsewhere, but it does not specify mainland China contact numbers.

Elsewhere, use independently confirmed police and emergency services appropriate to the location.
