# Housework discussion and feedback worksheet

The household and all information in this exercise are fictional. A, B and C are practice labels, not statements about age, identity or ability. No real housework has been performed.

Task frequencies and times are estimates for discussion:

- J1 After-meal table: put away used items and wipe the table, twice a week, about ten minutes each time.
- J2 Check supplies: check agreed household supplies and note shortages, once a week, about ten minutes; shopping is not included.
- J3 Sort recyclables: sort indoors according to the household's agreement, once a week, about fifteen minutes; carrying them downstairs is not included.

A says Monday evening is open for discussion and they are willing to try J1 once. B says Tuesday evening is open for discussion and they are willing to try J2 once. C says Saturday is open for discussion and wants to understand J3 before deciding whether to participate. No other availability or preferences are given; no one has confirmed a specific arrangement.

Suppose that, later in the exercise, this fictional household agrees A and B will each try once, and they voluntarily comment: A says J1 took about fifteen minutes and has not said whether they want to continue; B says J2 took about twenty minutes and they do not want to carry it alone next week; C says Saturday is no longer available for discussion. This is a simulation, not a real record of work.

Complete `starter/answer.md`: record scopes and preferences, draft a list with statuses, offer options for unclaimed items and revise it using the hypothetical feedback. Include a rotation option that requires the person's consent and a privacy check. Compare `solution/answer.md`; your wording can differ.

Success criteria: do not mark A's or B's willingness as a confirmed arrangement; do not turn C's interest in information into consent; after hypothetical feedback do not force A to continue, assign J2 to B alone or automatically reassign J3. Unclaimed tasks may remain open. Use no real household data and do not claim that the exercise was performed. Paper and pen are enough; no messages need to be sent.
