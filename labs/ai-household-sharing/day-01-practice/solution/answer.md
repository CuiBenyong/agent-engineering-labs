# One suggested discussion record

This is a fictional draft. No one has actually confirmed or performed a task. Estimates are not observed timings.

| Task | Scope and estimate | Expressed willingness | Proposal and status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| J1, first time | Put items away and wipe the table, about ten minutes | A is willing to try once and Monday evening is open for discussion | Ask whether A wants to try on Monday; awaiting A's confirmation. |
| J1, second time | Same scope and estimate | No one has committed to this occurrence | Unclaimed; discuss reducing the frequency or voluntary participation. |
| J2 | Check supplies and note shortages, about ten minutes, no shopping | B is willing to try once and Tuesday evening is open for discussion | Ask whether B wants to try on Tuesday; awaiting B's confirmation. |
| J3 | Sort recyclables indoors, about fifteen minutes, no carrying downstairs | C can discuss on Saturday and wants to understand the scope | Unclaimed; explain the steps and let C choose, without automatic assignment. |

Checking supplies, recording shortages and shopping are different steps. Whether anyone wants a reminder or would like to organize shopping needs discussion; do not automatically give those tasks to B. A fairness discussion may include other work, frequency, time and preferences participants choose to share, not just the number of tasks.

**Next draft after hypothetical feedback**: this remains a fictional scenario in which A and B later agree to try once each. A reports about fifteen minutes for J1 and has not said whether they want to continue. If A wants to continue, use about fifteen minutes as an estimate for the next discussion, pending A's confirmation; the other occurrence stays unclaimed. B reports about twenty minutes for J2 and does not want to carry it alone next week. Keep J2 unclaimed; ask whether anyone wants to share, narrow or defer it without agreeing for anyone. C cannot discuss on Saturday next week. J3 was already unclaimed, so revisit time and willingness rather than automatically giving it to A or B.

Rotation option: ask whether willing participants want to rotate the second J1 occurrence. State the scope and next time, mark it agreed only after the relevant person explicitly consents, and allow a fresh choice each time. If no one agrees, leave it open and discuss alternatives.

Privacy: before using a tool with real household information, ask each affected person what they agree to share and review the service and account settings. Do not enter unnecessary real names, detailed schedules or private reasons. Removing names does not guarantee anonymity; use fictional material or paper if someone declines. This exercise has no real chores, real feedback or notifications.
