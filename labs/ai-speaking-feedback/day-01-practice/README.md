# Speaking feedback and repeat practice

Imagine a fictional small group. You want to invite everyone to begin a discussion by each sharing a book they recently read. First say two or three sentences in your own words. Promptly write what you actually said in `starter/answer.md` and mark anything you cannot recall accurately as “uncertain.” If you cannot speak yet, write a draft and mark “not yet practiced aloud”; return to the speaking step later. Do not present the draft as a measured performance.

Record the audience, purpose, and clarity and structure questions you want reviewed. You may use an AI text tool or write suggestions yourself with help from the lesson. Check whether every suggestion quotes the original words. Explain why you accept, adapt, reject, or need to confirm it; revise in your own words, speak the whole message again, and record actual observations. If you have not repeated the practice, write “not yet practiced again”; do not invent an outcome.

Use fictional material. Paper is enough, and you do not need to upload a recording, real names, or private conversations. `solution/answer.md` is a paper simulation, not a test of a real learner or service.
