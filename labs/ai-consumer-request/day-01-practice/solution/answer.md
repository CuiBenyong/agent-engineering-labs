# Sample answer: after-sales communication preparation

This is a paper answer to a fictional situation. There was no real transaction, image, seller response or sending, and it is not a legal conclusion. Other wording can work if it states the person's request accurately without inventing facts.

## 1. Fictional facts, material and unknowns

- S1: one ceramic mug ordered on 8 October 2030.
- S2: package received on 10 October 2030.
- S3: on opening it on 11 October 2030, the person saw a crack where the handle joins the cup; it has not been used.
- S4: suppose a photo was taken after opening that shows the crack. There is no real image file here; it cannot establish when or why the crack formed and was not uploaded.
- S5: the seller has not been contacted and has not replied.
- Cause, seller policy, replacement and refund availability, materials, costs and timing are unknown. Transport damage is a possible guess, not an established scenario fact.

## 2. Review the imaginary flawed draft

| Draft claim | Scenario comparison | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Damage confirmed on receipt day | S2 is receipt on 10 October; S3 is observation on opening on 11 October | Keep the dates and observation separate |
| Carrier broke it | Cause unknown | Remove the asserted cause; say it is unknown if useful |
| Seller agreed to a full refund in seven days | S5 says no contact or response | Remove the promise and ask what the person wants to know |
| Order number and phone are attached | No real attachment or contact details exist here | Do not invent attachments or numbers or upload real personal information |
| Please handle it immediately | A person may request this, but no deadline was agreed in the scenario | Ask about steps and estimated time without inventing a seller promise |

## 3. Request and unsent draft

- First question: whether the same model can be replaced.
- If replacement is unavailable: whether a refund can be requested, plus required materials, steps, possible costs and estimated time.
- Reusable request:

> Use only the fictional mug scenario to draft an unsent consumer message. Preserve order, receipt and opening-observation dates, the unknown cause and the lack of seller response. Ask first about replacement of the same model and, if unavailable, whether a refund can be requested, with materials, steps, possible costs and timing. Do not invent responsibility, a seller promise, order number or legal conclusion. Do not upload material or send a message.

Unsent draft:

> Hello, I ordered a ceramic mug on 8 October 2030 and received the package on 10 October. When I opened it on 11 October, I saw a crack where the handle joins the cup; I have not used it. I do not know what caused the crack. Could you tell me whether the same model can be replaced? If replacement is not possible, I would also like to ask whether I can request a refund. What materials and steps would be needed, and what costs and estimated time might be involved?

## 4. Personal checks and status

- In a real case the person checks the actual product, dates, observation and whether a suitable photo exists. Fictional S4 is not a submitted real image.
- Do not upload real order numbers, addresses, phone numbers, chat records, screenshots, photos or other personal information to AI. If a real verification is later needed, personally check the actual channel and necessity of information first.
- In this exercise the seller has not been contacted or replied, no attachment was actually sent and no refund was approved; the draft remains unsent.
- Cause, seller rules and replacement or refund outcome remain unknown. This exercise does not decide responsibility or actual legal rights.
