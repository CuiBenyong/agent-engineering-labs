# Sample answer: two text alternatives

This answer uses the fictional fact card. There is no real picture, model image analysis or screen-reader test. Other wording can work if it matches the facts and the actual context.

## 1. Fictional facts and unknowns

- F1: One person sits at a wooden table by a window, with both hands holding an open book.
- F2: One blue cup is to the right of the book.
- F3: There is no discernible text in the image.
- F4: The scenario does not provide the person's name, age, occupation, identity or emotional state.

## 2. Two contexts

Context A is a reading-space article that needs the relationship between the person and book at a window-side table, based on F1:

> A person sits at a table by a window, holding an open book with both hands.

Context B explains table arrangement and needs the blue cup's position to the right of the book, based on F1 and F2:

> A blue cup is to the right of an open book.

Neither sentence guesses identity or text. A emphasizes the person and book; B emphasizes the cup's position relative to the book. Whether other details belong depends on the real purpose and surrounding text; there is no fixed sentence for every setting.
