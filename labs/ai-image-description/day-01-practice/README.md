# Fictional scene text-alternative practice

Complete `starter/answer.md` on paper or in a document, then compare `solution/answer.md`. The fact card describes a nonexistent practice image. There is no real person, picture, model image analysis or screen-reader test; no image upload or generation is needed.

## Fictional fact card

- F1: One person sits at a wooden table by a window, with both hands holding an open book.
- F2: One blue cup is to the right of the book.
- F3: There is no discernible text in the image.
- F4: The scenario does not provide the person's name, age, occupation, identity or emotional state.

Two contexts use the same fictional image. A is an article about a reading space, where the image adds the relationship between the person, book and window-side table. B explains table arrangement, where the image shows the blue cup to the right of the book. Nearby text in each context does not already provide the relationship it needs.

Imaginary flawed draft: “A happy retired teacher reads a story at home, beside a poster announcing reading-club registration.” It was invented to expose unsupported identity, emotion, location and text, not observed as a model output.

## Four steps

1. List the supplied objects, actions and positions and the unknowns that must not be guessed.
2. Write one concise alternative for A and one for B, identifying the facts used and why each detail matters. You need not mechanically list every detail.
3. Mark the flawed draft's unsupported age, occupation, emotion, home setting, story and poster text; explain how to correct it.
4. Record real-use checks for context, image text, privacy permission and actual assistive-technology presentation. Mark this exercise as having no real page or test.

## Completion criteria

A conveys the person and book at the window-side table; B conveys the blue cup to the right of the book. Neither guesses identity or nonexistent text. Explain why purpose changes the emphasis, recognize that decorative, functional and complex images require context-specific decisions, and do not call a paper description a completed accessibility test.
