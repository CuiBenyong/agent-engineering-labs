# One suggested scaling record

- Factor: target six servings ÷ original four servings = **1.5**, assuming the same portion size.

| Ingredient | Original for four | Calculation | Theoretical result for six |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cooked and drained beans | 400 grams | 400 × 1.5 | 600 grams, still measured cooked and drained |
| Carrots | 300 grams | 300 × 1.5 | 450 grams |
| Water | 400 milliliters | 400 × 1.5 | 600 milliliters, or 0.6 liters |
| Cooking oil | 2 teaspoons | 2 × 1.5 | 3 teaspoons, or 15 milliliters under the exercise convention |
| Tomatoes | 1 | 1 × 1.5 | 1.5 tomatoes |
| Salt | 2 grams | 2 × 1.5 | 3 grams |

With one teaspoon defined as 5 milliliters here, the oil for six is 3 × 5 = 15 milliliters. Alternatively, scale the original 10 milliliters by 1.5. The water is 600 ÷ 1,000 = 0.6 liters. The 600 grams of beans can also be written as 0.6 kilograms, but cannot be relabeled as dry-bean weight.

In the incorrect draft, 800 grams of cooked beans doubles the original rather than multiplying by 1.5. Three teaspoons are 15 milliliters under the exercise convention, not 30. Two tomatoes are not exactly 1.5 times one tomato. Preserve 1.5 as the theoretical result; if shopping requires buying two whole tomatoes, keep purchase and actual use separate. The cook decides and records any change; the exercise does not establish tomato weight.

Three grams of salt is a proportional result, not personalized health advice or a mandatory amount. The cook may separately decide and record a taste adjustment. Before preparing actual food, check the specific product's label and cross-contact information, follow applicable personal care arrangements and consult reliable preparation, heating and storage guidance. Seek a qualified professional's help when needed. Arithmetic does not guarantee absence of allergens or suitability for anyone, and heating time does not automatically multiply by 1.5.
