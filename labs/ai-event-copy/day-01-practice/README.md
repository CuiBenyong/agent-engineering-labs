# Fictional shop event-copy review

Complete `starter/answer.md` on paper or in a document, then compare `solution/answer.md`. The event, records and drafts are fictional. No AI use, upload, message sending or payment is required.

## Scenario records

- E1 schedule: 14 November 2030, 14:00 to 15:00. No weekday is given.
- E2 activity: a postcard-writing experience inside a fictional shop.
- E3 participant entitlement: each participant may use one blank postcard provided by the shop and take home the postcard they wrote after the activity.
- The exercise assumes a fictional organizer confirmed E1, E2 and E3. Fee, price, purchase requirement, booking method, capacity, eligibility, street address and contact method remain unconfirmed.
- Two channels: a community message for people who may be interested, and an in-store notice for shop customers. Both aim to explain the activity and, once conditions are confirmed, help readers know how to participate.
- Imaginary flawed draft: “Join free on 15 November, no booking needed. Everyone gets two blank postcards; unlimited places.” This was written for practice, not observed as model output.

## Four steps

1. Identify the audience for each channel and the shared purpose. Record the three source cards and at least four open questions.
2. Write a reusable AI request and a headline plus body for each channel. Cite source IDs for every sentence and label both versions internal drafts.
3. Split the flawed draft into date, free entry, no booking, two postcards and unlimited places. Give the evidence or gap, decision and reason for each.
4. Check date, fee and complete entitlement; explain why publication is on hold and what the organizer must confirm.

## Completion criteria

Each channel has a headline and body with traceable facts. Correct the date from E1 and the complete entitlement from E3; do not turn unknown fee, booking or capacity into facts. Both drafts remain on hold until information important to readers' actions is confirmed and reviewed again. The exercise does not describe a real event or a sent message.
