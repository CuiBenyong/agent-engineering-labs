# Fictional reading-event survey design

Complete `starter/answer.md` on paper or in a document, then compare `solution/answer.md`. The event, intended audience and walkthrough situations are fictional. No AI call, survey distribution, contact with others or collection of real personal information is required.

## Scenario and flawed draft

Imagine that a reading event invited a group of readers. Some did not attend, and some attendees did not join the discussion. The purpose is to understand feelings about the discussion among those who joined it and interest in activities for a similar future event among invited readers. Every item may be skipped. This exercise does not ask for names, contact details or other identity information.

Three deliberately flawed draft items to revise:

1. “At this wonderful and inspiring event, weren't you very satisfied with both the discussion and the venue?” The only choices are “satisfied” and “very satisfied.”
2. “Which activity do you like most?” allows only one choice between “reader sharing” and “small-group discussion.”
3. “Please provide your name and contact details so we can notify you later.”

## Four steps

1. State the invited audience and purpose. Identify leading wording, a combined question, missing options and unnecessary identity information in the three flawed items.
2. Write a short survey with at least three items, explaining single choice, multiple choice, applicable and not-applicable situations, cannot evaluate, prefer not to answer and skipping. If you use an open item, tell readers not to enter personal information. State that mutually exclusive multiple-choice options are selected alone.
3. Walk through three imaginary situations on paper: attended discussion and felt neutral, knows they did not attend, and prefers not to reveal or rate. Record at least two risks and revisions without treating imagined choices as real data.
4. List permissions, data handling and actual form settings an owner would need to confirm before use. Explain that this exercise was not distributed, has no real results and cannot establish representativeness.

## Completion criteria

Questions are neutral and ask one thing at a time. People who can rate, did not participate, cannot evaluate or prefer not to answer have appropriate paths. Multiple interests can be selected together and exclusive options cannot be combined with others. Every item is skippable; no real personal information is collected. Walkthroughs and revisions are imaginary, with no invented survey results.
