# Sample answer: survey and paper walkthroughs

This answer uses a fictional scenario only. No real distribution, respondents, personal information or survey results exist. Other wording can work if it preserves the same answer paths and boundaries.

## 1. Audience, purpose and flawed draft

- Audience: readers invited to the fictional reading event. Some may not have attended, and some attendees may not have joined the discussion.
- Purpose: understand discussion feelings among those who joined it and invited readers' interest in activities for a similar future event.
- Flawed item 1 uses “wonderful” and “inspiring” and a leading question to presume a positive view, combines discussion with venue, offers only positive responses and lacks a not-applicable path.
- Flawed item 2 forces one choice between sharing and discussion and cannot express interest in both, another activity, no interest or uncertainty.
- Flawed item 3 asks for names and contact details although identity is unnecessary for this exercise's purpose.

## 2. Short survey draft

Introduction: Participation is voluntary; every question may be skipped. Do not enter names, contact details or other identifying information.

**Q1 | Did you attend this reading event?** (Choose one; you may also skip.)

- Attended
- Did not attend
- Do not remember
- Prefer not to answer

**Q2 | How did you feel overall about the discussion part of this event?** (Choose one; you may also skip.)

- Very satisfied
- Somewhat satisfied
- Neither satisfied nor dissatisfied
- Somewhat dissatisfied
- Very dissatisfied
- Not applicable: I know I did not attend the event or did not join the discussion
- Do not remember or cannot evaluate for another reason
- Prefer not to answer

Choose not applicable if you know you did not attend or join the discussion; use the next option if you do not remember or cannot evaluate for another reason. You may instead prefer not to answer or skip.

**Q3 | If a similar reading event takes place in the future, which activities interest you?** (Select all that apply; select any of the last three options on its own; you may also skip.)

- Reader sharing
- Small-group discussion
- Quiet reading
- Another activity (no explanation required)
- Not interested in any activity (select on its own)
- Unsure (select on its own)
- Prefer not to answer (select on its own)

**Q4 | If you wish, what is one event arrangement you would like to change?** (You may skip; do not include names, contact details or other identifying information.)

Reusable request:

> Draft a short survey for readers invited to a fictional reading event; some did not attend or join discussion. Ask about discussion feelings and future activity interests, one neutral topic at a time. Specify single and multiple choice, not applicable, cannot evaluate, prefer not to answer and skipping. Select exclusive multiple-choice options alone. Do not request names or contact details or invent responses or results. Provide a paper draft for human review.

## 3. Imaginary paper walkthroughs and revisions

| Imaginary situation | Possible choices or skip path | Risk and revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Joined discussion, felt neutral and likes two activities | Q1 attended; Q2 neither satisfied nor dissatisfied; Q3 may select reader sharing and small-group discussion | Original single choice could not express two interests; allow multiple choices |
| Knows they did not attend | Q1 did not attend; Q2 not applicable; Q3 may reflect future interest or be skipped | Original assumed experience and offered only positive choices; add not applicable, neutral and negative options |
| Prefers not to disclose attendance or provide a rating | Choose prefer not to answer or leave Q1 and Q2 blank | Do not force a response; allow every item to be skipped |

- A specific interest selected alongside “not interested in any activity” is a conflict. Check the instruction and flag the issue; do not guess the person's intent.
- Remove “wonderful” and the combined question; ask about discussion alone, and write a separate venue item only if needed.
- Remove the name and contact request. A real open response can still contain personal information and needs an appropriate handling plan before use.

## 4. Boundaries and status

- Before real use, the responsible person confirms the audience, necessary permissions, privacy handling, whether the form can implement exclusive choices and skipping, and whether a real pilot is needed.
- This exercise has no distribution, real responses or personal-information collection. The three cases are designer-created walkthroughs, not three respondents.
- A paper walkthrough can expose obvious answer-path gaps; it cannot establish that real readers understood, an actual satisfaction rate or statistical representativeness.
