# D4: Trace claims to evidence

Review a fictional draft against the given material. Lin has one hour on Friday; public event material is organized; next week's plans were discussed with a colleague but not decided; the event venue is unconfirmed. These are exercise premises, not proof of a real event.

Paper, a phone note, or a computer document is enough. An assistant is optional. No purchase, real personal information, real venue contact, or external message is required.

## Steps

1. Copy [the starter](starter/exercise.md). Read the fictional draft and split mixed statements so facts and suggestions receive separate rows.
2. For each claim, record the supporting source quotation or “not supplied,” choose **supported**, **conflict**, **unknown**, or **suggestion**, and say what to do with it. A suggestion is not a completed action.
3. Correct the conflicting plan status. Remove unsupported claims about price and opening hours, or clearly leave those details unknown. Save a revised paragraph retaining all three source statuses.
4. Write an independent verification plan for a future real need: first identify a relevant responsible operator if one can be established, then use its current official source and check the applicable place, conditions, and date of access. Repeating the same question to an assistant is not an independent source.
5. Label any steps you did not perform **plan, not performed**. Do not invent a venue, page, visit, response, price, or opening time. If there is no identifiable venue or responsible source, leave the issue unresolved.
6. Compare your record with [one possible solution](solution/exercise.md).

## Acceptance

- Every part of the draft has a claim, basis or explicit absence of one, judgment, and handling; mixed statements are split.
- The categories and reasons distinguish supported, conflicting, unknown, and suggested content.
- The revision retains organized material, discussed-but-undecided plans, and an unconfirmed venue without unsupported additions.
- The independent check names a feasible future source and what evidence to record; unperformed work is not called verified. Other reasoned answers are acceptable.
